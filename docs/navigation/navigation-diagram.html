How to read the map

The map runs in four sections down the page, ordered by how much access a page needs. The top section is open to anyone; each one below asks for one more thing – an account, then a login, then an admin role. Pages are named the way a person would describe them; the web address of every one is in the last table.

Pages worth explaining

Most pages are simple: one job, one way in, one way out. These are not, either because they do several things at once, because something is built into them, or because not everyone may open them.

The home page holds everything the public sees on one screen. Membership, Lessons, Schedule, FAQ and Contact in the top bar do not open new pages – they scroll you down to a section of this same one, and on a phone they hide behind the menu button. Signing in is built into that top bar as well: logged out you see Log in and Sign up, and once you are logged in both disappear and a Dashboard button takes their place.

Log in asks for your email, then your password, one step at a time. There are three ways out of it – your account if the details are right, the sign-up page, or the reset-password page. Choosing Google instead sends you through a hidden checking step before you land anywhere.

Sign up does not sign you in, which surprises people. It emails you a confirmation link and sends you to the login page. Only when you click the link in that email does the hidden confirm step run and drop you on your account page.

Set a new password has no button anywhere that opens it. Forgot password emails you a link, and that link is the only way in. That is why the arrow leading to it on the map is dotted rather than solid.

Confirm email link and Google sign-in check never show you anything. They read the code in your link, check it with Supabase, and move you straight on – to your account if the code was good, to the error page if it was not.

Your account is the junction of the whole site. Every way of signing in ends here: password, Google, a confirmed email, or a freshly reset password. It also carries the top bar with the Members link, which is the only route from the member side into the admin side.

My profile lets you change your name and phone number, but your email and your role are locked – the server throws those away even if the form is tampered with. Nothing links to this page yet, so for now you reach it by typing the address.

The members list looks like several pages but is only one. Searching by name, filtering by role or status, and moving between pages all change the web address while keeping you here. Two things lead away from it: the Add member button, and clicking any row.

Member details does two jobs on one page. It edits a member, and it switches them between active and inactive. Deactivating is not deleting – the member keeps their history and simply stops being included in invoicing.

The two checkpoints are not pages, and nobody clicks them. They run before a page loads: ask for a member page without being logged in and you are sent to Log in; ask for an admin page as an ordinary member and you are sent back to your own account. Both happen on the server, before anything is drawn.

The grey pages are the ones from our Milestone 2 design that we have not built. They are on the map so it lines up with the plan we submitted, and drawn in grey dashes so nobody mistakes them for working pages.
